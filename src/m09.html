Spend, stock and supplier playbooks

Module 8 covered control checks: duplicates, splits, vendor records. This module covers the other half of a purchasing auditor's job: where the money goes, whether stock moving between warehouses arrives, what's sitting unused, and which suppliers deliver as promised.

About 40 minutes. Level: Intermediate.

After this module you should be able to:

  • Classify messy spend data and find spend that is coded to the wrong account
  • Reconcile transfers between warehouses and explain stock "lost in transit"
  • Identify slow-moving, obsolete and excess stock by warehouse
  • Measure supplier delivery performance (OTIF) and price variance

All four playbooks use the practice data pack: fictional data for seven warehouses with problems planted in it. If you haven't downloaded it yet, get it from the Practice data page. The assignment at the end of this module checks your answers against it.

P7 · Spend classification and analysis

GoalKnow what you buy, from whom, and whether it's coded correctly
WhenQuarterly
DataAP lines with supplier, description, amount and GL code (spend_lines.csv)
OutputSpend by category and supplier, miscoding list, savings ideas

Spend analysis is the most common use of AI in procurement. Indirect spend (things you buy that aren't stock: services, repairs, utilities, labour) is usually the messiest, because descriptions are free text and people pick whatever GL code gets the invoice paid. A large "Sundry" or "Miscellaneous" balance is a sign that nobody really knows what was bought.

  1. Agree a short category list. 8 to 15 categories that mean something to your business, for example Packaging, MHE (forklift) maintenance, Facility services, Utilities, Temporary labour, Freight, IT, PPE. Keep it short enough to remember.
  2. Classify every line with AI. Use the prompt below. Ask for a confidence level, and review every "Low".
  3. Spot-check 20 lines across categories. If more than two are wrong, refine the category definitions and run again.
  4. Pivot. Spend by category, by supplier within category, and by GL code against category.
  5. Look for the stories: spend coded to Sundry; one category spread across many small suppliers (a chance to consolidate); categories with no contract; month-on-month jumps.
Classify each spend line into exactly one of these categories:
Packaging materials | MHE maintenance & parts | Facility services | Utilities | Temporary labour | Freight & courier | IT & telecoms | PPE & safety | Other
Use the description and supplier name. Descriptions are messy and may be in capitals or abbreviated (e.g. "FLT" = forklift, "LOLER" = lifting equipment inspection).
Using code, return: Line_ID, Category, Confidence (High/Low). Then give me:
1. Total spend by category, largest first.
2. Total spend currently coded to GL 6999 Sundry, and how it splits across categories.
3. For each category, the number of suppliers and their share of spend.
Tell me how many lines you classified and the overall total so I can tie it out.
Why ask for a total

Classification can silently drop lines. If the category totals don't add up to the file total, something was missed.

P8 · Inter-warehouse transfer reconciliation

GoalMake sure stock sent between warehouses arrives, in full
WhenWeekly or monthly
DataTransfer log (transfers.csv) plus count results
OutputOpen and short transfers, by route and age

With seven warehouses, stock moves around constantly. Each transfer has two halves: the sending site ships it and the receiving site books it in. Problems show up in three ways:

  • Shipped but never received. Stock is "in transit" in the system for weeks. It may be lost, or received physically but not booked.
  • Received short. Fewer units booked in than were shipped.
  • Moved but never recorded. Someone moves stock without a transfer at all. The counts then show a shortage at one site and a matching surplus at another, for the same SKU, with no transfer record. This is the "opposite variances" pattern from Module 6.
  1. Age the open transfers. Days since ship date for every transfer without a receipt. Anything over your normal transit time (say 7 days) needs chasing; over 14 days is an exception.
  2. Compare shipped vs received quantities for completed transfers.
  3. Cross-check counts. For SKUs short at one site and over at another, check whether a transfer explains it.
  4. Summarise by route (from → to). Repeated problems on one route usually point to one site's receiving process.
Using code on transfers.csv (snapshot date 31 October 2026):
1. List transfers with no Receipt_Date that were shipped more than 14 days before the snapshot date, with days outstanding.
2. List completed transfers where Qty_Received is less than Qty_Shipped, with the shortfall.
3. Summarise both by route (From_WH to To_WH).
Then, using cycle_counts.csv: find SKUs that are short in one warehouse and over by a similar quantity in another within 3 days, and say whether a transfer in transfers.csv explains it.

P9 · Slow-moving, obsolete and excess stock

GoalFind money tied up in stock that isn't being used
WhenQuarterly, and before year end
DataStock on hand with last movement date and usage (stock_on_hand.csv)
OutputAgeing by warehouse, obsolete list, excess list, actions

Stock that doesn't move costs money: storage space, handling, insurance, and eventually a write-off. It is also a purchasing issue, because excess usually starts with buying too much. Two measures matter:

MeasureHow to calculateTypical rule
Age since last movementSnapshot date minus last movement date, in daysBuckets: 0–90, 91–180, 181–365, over 365 days. Over 365 with no usage is usually treated as obsolete.
Months of supplyQuantity on hand ÷ average monthly usageOver 12 months is usually treated as excess (set your own threshold)
  1. Add the two calculated columns (ask AI for the formulas, or have it do it with code).
  2. Value each bucket by warehouse. A PivotTable of Stock_Value by WH and age bucket.
  3. List the top items by value in the over-365 and excess groups.
  4. Agree actions with warehouse and category managers: transfer to a site that uses it, return to supplier, sell, or write off. Also stop re-ordering it.
Using code on stock_on_hand.csv (snapshot date 31 October 2026):
1. Add Age_Days (snapshot minus Last_Movement_Date) and Age_Bucket (0-90, 91-180, 181-365, >365).
2. Add Months_Of_Supply = Qty_On_Hand / Avg_Monthly_Usage (show "No usage" when usage is 0).
3. Table: Stock_Value by WH and Age_Bucket, with totals.
4. List lines over 365 days, largest value first, and lines with more than 12 months of supply.
5. Which warehouse has the most value over 365 days?
Give me the total Stock_Value so I can tie it out.

P10 · Supplier delivery performance and price variance

GoalKnow which suppliers deliver as promised, at the agreed price
WhenMonthly or quarterly supplier reviews
DataPOs with promised dates, goods receipts, invoices
OutputOTIF by supplier, price variance list, review agenda

OTIF means "on time, in full": the share of orders delivered by the promised date, in the quantity ordered (and, for this course, in the unit ordered). It's the standard supplier delivery measure. Purchase price variance (PPV) is the difference between what you paid and what you agreed, either the PO price or the contract price.

  1. Join POs to goods receipts on PO number. A line is on time if GRN date ≤ promised date, and in full if quantity received ≥ quantity ordered.
  2. Calculate OTIF by supplier. Ignore suppliers with very few orders (fewer than 5), where one late delivery swings the figure.
  3. Join invoices to POs and calculate price variance per line and in total by supplier.
  4. Prepare a review agenda for the worst performers: facts first, then questions.
Using code on po_register.csv, grn_register.csv and invoice_register.csv:
1. Join POs to GRNs on PO_No. A PO is On_Time if GRN_Date <= Promised_Date, In_Full if Qty_Received >= Qty and the GRN UOM equals Order_UOM.
2. OTIF % by Vendor_ID for vendors with at least 5 received POs, worst first. Also overall OTIF.
3. Join invoices to POs and calculate price variance per line ((Invoice Unit_Price − PO Unit_Price) × Qty) and the total by vendor.
4. For the three worst OTIF vendors, draft a short supplier review agenda with the facts and 5 questions.

Summary

  • Spend analysis: short category list, AI classification with confidence, spot-check, then pivot. Watch the Sundry balance.
  • Transfers have two halves. Age the open ones, and match opposite count variances to transfers.
  • Age since last movement and months of supply find obsolete and excess stock.
  • OTIF and price variance make supplier reviews factual.
