Module 10 of 14 · Part 3: AI for your audit work

Case study: a month-end audit

A walk-through of one complete October audit of the practice data, from receiving the files to sending the summary. It shows how the pieces from modules 3 to 9 fit together, and the points where AI got things wrong and how they were caught.

About 45 minutes. Level: Intermediate to advanced.

After this module you should be able to:

  • Plan a month-end audit across seven warehouses as a sequence of steps
  • Recognise the typical ways AI output goes wrong on real files, and catch them
  • Decide which findings matter most and present them in order

The setting: it's the first week of November 2026. You have October's data from seven warehouses and five working days to produce the monthly audit pack. Every number below comes from the practice data, so you can reproduce each step yourself. The assignment at the end asks you to.

Day 1 · Collect, combine and tie out

The seven warehouses send their count files. You put them in one folder and combine them with Power Query (Module 7).

You

[Uploads the 7 warehouse files] Combine these and tell me the row count per warehouse.

AI

Combined 7 files, 832 rows. WH04 has 113 rows…

Your Power Query result says 829. WH04's file has a title line and a blank row above the headers, and the AI counted them as data. If you had accepted 832, every later percentage for WH04 would have been slightly wrong. This is why step one is always a control total from a source you trust.
You

WH04's file has a title row and a blank row above the header. Skip them and recount.

AI

Corrected: 829 rows. WH04 has 110.

Lesson

AI handles the common case well and the messy case badly. One file in seven with a slightly different layout is normal in real life. Tie out every time.

AI

WH07 shows shortages on high-value items, suggesting possible theft.

The pattern is real, but "theft" is a conclusion the data can't support. Four one-directional shortages on portable, high-value items is a red flag for a recount and a security review, not an accusation. The finding should say what the data shows.
AI

WH04 has a net variance close to zero for SKU-10219, so it's accurate.

Net zero hides two large errors that cancel out. Gross variance and line-level accuracy show what's going on, which is why Module 6 told you to track both.

Day 3 · Purchase to pay

You run Playbooks P2 (duplicates), P3 (split POs) and a three-way match check on the PO, GRN and invoice registers.

  • Five near-duplicate invoices worth $26,100: a letter suffix (33071 / 33071A), leading zeros (INV-00451 / 451), transposed digits (88213 / 88231), the same invoice entered under a duplicate supplier record (V009 / V049), and the same amount three days apart.
  • Four split-PO clusters, all by requester R07 at WH03: 11 POs between $4,600 and $4,990, $52,332 in total, against a $5,000 approval limit.
  • Six invoices with no PO totalling $51,950. Three, worth $43,950, are from V051, a supplier created on 22 September.
  • Four invoices charging for more than was received, five priced more than 2% above the PO, two dated before their PO, and one PO for 10 cases received as 120 eaches.
AI

I also found 11 further pairs with the same supplier and amount within 30 days.

You check a sample: they are repeat orders with their own POs and receipts. The prompt's rule (a) was too broad on its own. You refine it: same supplier and amount, AND the same PO or no PO. The real list drops to the five.

Day 4 · Vendors, transfers, stock health, suppliers

  • Vendor master: three duplicate pairs (V009/V049, V022/V050, V015/V044), five records without a tax ID, seven dormant suppliers. V051 stands out: new, no tax ID, PO box address, $43,950 invoiced, none of it on a PO. That combination goes to your manager today, not in the monthly pack.
  • Transfers: five shipped more than 14 days ago and never received; three received short. The WH02/WH06 opposite variances have no transfer record at all, so stock was moved without one.
  • Stock health: 34 lines worth $539,007 haven't moved in over a year, most of it in WH07. 26 lines hold more than 12 months of supply.
  • Suppliers: overall OTIF is 88.1%; V012 is at 31.2%.
  • Spend: $161,600 of $645,161 indirect spend is coded to 6999 Sundry. Temporary labour is the largest category.

October inventory and purchasing audit: 7 warehouses

Network stock accuracy was 93.5% against a 98% target (54 of 829 count lines outside tolerance). Most of the $181,984 gross variance is stock in the wrong place: net variance is −$13,405.

Escalated separately today: supplier V051 (created 22 Sep, no tax ID, PO box address) has been paid $43,950 on three invoices with no purchase orders.

Top findings
1. Approval limit bypassed: 11 POs by one requester at WH03, each just under $5,000, total $52,332.
2. Duplicate invoices: 5 pairs, $26,100, recoverable. Matching rules need tightening for suffixes and duplicate supplier records.
3. WH05 accuracy 87.1%: 14 errors are case/each mix-ups. Fix: receiving screen default unit.
4. $539,007 of stock (34 lines, mostly WH07) has not moved for over a year.
5. 5 transfers not received after 14+ days; 3 SKUs moved WH02→WH06 with no transfer posted.

Decisions needed: approve recount at WH07 and supplier review with V012 (OTIF 31.2%).
What made this work

AI did the searching, joining and first drafts in minutes. You caught four AI errors: a miscounted file, an unsupported "theft" conclusion, a net figure hiding errors, and 11 false duplicates. Each was caught by the same habit: check totals, look at the underlying rows, and only state what the data supports.

Summary

  • A month-end audit is a sequence: tie out, accuracy, purchase-to-pay, vendors and stock health, prioritise, write up.
  • Expect AI to stumble on messy files, overreach on conclusions and over-flag. Your checks catch it.
  • Rank findings by money, control weakness and urgency. Escalate urgent fraud indicators immediately.
  • A one-page summary with decisions needed beats a long list.
