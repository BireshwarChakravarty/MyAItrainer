Your AI toolbox

There are hundreds of AI products. For your work they fall into four groups, and knowing which group a tool belongs to tells you what it's good for.

About 20 minutes. Level: Beginner.

After this module you should be able to:

  • Name the four layers of AI tools and give an example of each
  • Choose the right tool for a given audit or procurement task
  • Know which features matter most for data work, and what to ask IT

The four layers

Think of them as moving from "general and flexible" to "specialised and automatic".

  1. Layer 1Chat assistantsA general assistant you talk to in a browser or app. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot Chat.
  2. Layer 2AI inside your appsThe Copilot or Gemini button inside Excel, Outlook, Teams, Word, Google Sheets.
  3. Layer 3AI in business systemsFeatures built into your ERP, WMS or procurement suite: invoice matching, spend classification, demand forecasts.
  4. Layer 4Automation & agentsFlows and agents that run multi-step tasks on a schedule or trigger, such as Power Automate.

Layer 1 · Chat assistants

The main general-purpose assistants are ChatGPT (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google) and Microsoft Copilot. For your purposes they are more alike than different. All of them can explain concepts, write and fix formulas, draft documents, and analyse uploaded files. Recent reviews generally describe ChatGPT as the broadest all-rounder, Claude as strong for long documents and careful writing, Copilot as the natural fit if your company runs on Microsoft 365, and Gemini as the fit for Google Workspace.

The best one for you is usually the one your company has approved, because that version comes with business data protections. More on that in Module 3.

Layer 2 · AI inside the apps you already use

This is where an auditor gets the most value, because your data already lives there.

  • Copilot in Excel can create formula columns, highlight, sort and filter, build PivotTables and charts, and explain what is in a sheet. In early 2026 Microsoft made Agent Mode generally available in Excel for Windows, Mac and the web. You describe a multi-step job in plain English ("build a summary sheet comparing variance by warehouse with a chart") and it builds and edits the workbook step by step.
  • The =COPILOT() function lets you use a plain-English prompt inside a cell. It is useful for classifying text, but Microsoft itself warns not to use it for tasks needing accuracy or reproducibility, such as calculations. Module 7 covers exactly when to use it.
  • Copilot in Outlook and Teams summarises long email threads and meetings, and drafts replies.
  • Gemini in Google Sheets and Gmail offers similar features for Google Workspace companies.

Layer 3 · AI in your business systems

ERP, WMS and procurement platforms (SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, Coupa and others) increasingly include AI features: automatic three-way matching, duplicate invoice detection, spend classification, risk-based cycle count suggestions. You typically don't configure these yourself, but you should know whether your company has them, because they change what you need to check manually.

Layer 4 · Automation and agents

Tools like Microsoft Power Automate run a sequence of steps on a schedule ("every Monday at 7am, collect the seven warehouse files, refresh the report, email me the exceptions"). AI agents go a step further and can plan and carry out multi-step tasks with some independence. You'll build up to this in Module 12.

Features that matter for data work

When you look at any assistant, these are the features that make the biggest difference to an auditor:

FeatureWhat it meansWhy you care
File upload & data analysisYou upload an Excel or CSV file and the assistant writes and runs code (usually Python) to analyse itCalculations are done by real code, not word prediction, so the maths is far more reliable
Projects / custom instructionsSave standing context: your role, your seven warehouses, your column names, your tolerancesYou stop re-explaining your job in every chat
Long contextHow much text it can consider at onceLets you work with long contracts, SOPs or large tables in one go
Web searchThe assistant can look things up online and cite sourcesUseful for market prices, supplier news and regulations. Always open the source.
ConnectorsLinks to your email, files, SharePoint or other systemsThe assistant can find the right document itself, within your permissions

Questions to ask your IT team

Before you go further, find out what you have. Send these questions to IT or your manager. It shows initiative and avoids using something you shouldn't.

Hi [Name],

I'm planning to use AI tools to speed up my warehouse data audits. Could you help me with a few questions?

1. Which AI assistants are approved for work use (e.g. Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT Enterprise/Team, Claude, Gemini)?
2. Do I have a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence, so I can use Copilot inside Excel?
3. Is there a policy on what data can be shared with AI tools? Where can I read it?
4. Do our ERP / WMS / procurement systems have any AI features switched on (e.g. automatic invoice matching, anomaly alerts)?
5. Is Power Automate available to me for scheduling simple workflows?

Thanks,
[Your name]

Summary

  • Four layers: chat assistants, AI inside apps, AI in business systems, and automation/agents.
  • For audit work, AI inside Excel and file-analysis in a chat assistant give the fastest wins.
  • The best tool is usually the one your company has approved and licensed.
  • "File upload and data analysis" matters most, because the maths is done by real code.
